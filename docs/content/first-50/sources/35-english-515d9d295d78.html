West Lake Vinegar Fish

A whole fish is gently poached until tender, then glossed with a sharp-sweet vinegar sauce scented with ginger and scallion. The sauce should cling lightly to the flesh without drowning it.

Ingredients

1 whole fish, about 2 lb, cleaned and scaled

1 ½ tsp salt, divided

2 oz ginger, peeled and sliced

4 stalk scallion, cut into 2-inch pieces

6 cup water, for poaching

3 tbsp rice vinegar, plus more if needed

2 tbsp sugar

1 ½ tbsp light soy sauce

2 tbsp shaoxing wine

1 tbsp cornstarch, mixed with water

3 tbsp water, for slurry

1 tbsp neutral oil, for finishing the sauce

Instructions

1

Pat the fish dry and make 2 to 3 shallow diagonal slashes on each side. Rub the fish with 1 teaspoon of the salt and let it sit while you make the sauce, about 10 minutes.

2

In a small bowl, whisk the rice vinegar, sugar, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, and remaining salt until the sugar dissolves. Stir the cornstarch and water together in a separate bowl.

3

Bring the water, about half of the ginger, and half of the scallion pieces to a simmer in a wide skillet or fish pan. Slide in the fish and cook at a gentle simmer, spooning the liquid over the top occasionally, until the flesh is opaque and flakes easily at the thickest point or the center reaches 145°F, 8 to 12 minutes depending on thickness.

4

Carefully transfer the fish to a serving platter and keep it warm.

5

Pour off all but about 1 tablespoon of the poaching liquid from the pan. Add the remaining ginger and scallion, then pour in the vinegar mixture and bring it to a simmer.

6

Stir the cornstarch slurry again and add it to the sauce. Cook, stirring, until the sauce turns glossy and lightly thickened enough to coat a spoon, about 1 minute.

7

Stir in the oil, taste the sauce, and add a little more vinegar if you want a sharper edge. Spoon the sauce over the fish and serve at once.
